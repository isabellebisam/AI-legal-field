Key Cases

Thaler v. Vidal, 43 F.4th 1207 (Fed. Cir. 2022), cert. denied, 143 S. Ct. 1783 (2023)

Holding that AI cannot be listed as inventor on patent applications or author for copyright purposes; human authorship required.

Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd., No. 3:23-cv-00201 (N.D. Cal. filed Jan. 13, 2023)

Artists challenging use of their work in training data for Stable Diffusion image generation AI.

Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI, Inc., No. 1:23-cv-00135 (D. Del. filed Feb. 3, 2023)

Copyright and trademark infringement claims against Stability AI for training on Getty's images.

The New York Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp., No. 1:23-cv-11195 (S.D.N.Y. filed Dec. 27, 2023)

Copyright infringement claims against OpenAI and Microsoft for training on NYT articles.

Authors Guild, Inc. v. OpenAI, Inc., No. 1:23-cv-08292 (S.D.N.Y. filed Sept. 19, 2023)

Class action by authors alleging copyright infringement for training ChatGPT on their books.

Authors Guild v. Google, Inc., 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015)

Holding that Google Books' scanning and indexing of copyrighted books constitutes fair use.

Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994)

Supreme Court decision on parody as fair use, establishing that transformative use is central to fair use analysis.

Copyright Office Guidance

US Copyright Office, "Copyright Registration Guidance: Works Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence" (2023)

Policy statement requiring human authorship for copyright registration and disclosure of AI-generated content.

Copyright Office AI Page

US Copyright Office, "Notice of Inquiry and Request for Comments on Copyright and Artificial Intelligence" (2023)

Soliciting public input on copyright issues raised by AI, including training, outputs, and liability.

NOI (PDF)

Academic Literature

AI and Copyright

Lemley, Mark A. & Casey, Bryan. "Fair Learning." Texas Law Review 99 (2021): 743-789.

Analysis of whether training AI on copyrighted works constitutes fair use.

Grimmelmann, James. "Copyright for Literate Robots." Iowa Law Review 101 (2016): 657-681.

Explores how copyright law should apply to machine learning systems that "read" copyrighted texts.

Samuelson, Pamela. "Generative AI Meets Copyright." Science 381.6654 (2023): 158-161.

Overview of copyright challenges posed by generative AI systems.

Bridy, Annemarie. "The Price of Closing the 'Value Gap': How the Music Industry Hacked EU Copyright Reform." Vanderbilt Journal of Entertainment & Technology Law 22 (2020): 323-360.

Analysis of automated copyright enforcement and platform liability in EU law.

Algorithmic Accountability and Fairness

Pasquale, Frank. The Black Box Society: The Secret Algorithms That Control Money and Information. Harvard University Press, 2015.

Foundational work on algorithmic opacity and the need for transparency in automated systems.

O'Neil, Cathy. Weapons of Math Destruction: How Big Data Increases Inequality and Threatens Democracy. Crown, 2016.

Accessible examination of how algorithmic systems can perpetuate discrimination and harm.

Crawford, Kate. Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence. Yale University Press, 2021.

Critical analysis of AI's social, political, and environmental implications.

Barocas, Solon, Moritz Hardt, and Arvind Narayanan. Fairness and Machine Learning: Limitations and Opportunities. MIT Press, 2023.

Comprehensive technical and conceptual treatment of algorithmic fairness.

Free online version

Raji, Inioluwa Deborah, et al. "Closing the AI Accountability Gap: Defining an End-to-End Framework for Internal Algorithmic Auditing." FAT* '20: Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (2020): 33-44.

Framework for conducting algorithmic audits to identify bias and ensure accountability.

Platform Governance and Content Moderation

Gillespie, Tarleton. Custodians of the Internet: Platforms, Content Moderation, and the Hidden Decisions That Shape Social Media. Yale University Press, 2018.

Examination of how platforms moderate content and the societal implications of these decisions.

Roberts, Sarah T. Behind the Screen: Content Moderation in the Shadows of Social Media. Yale University Press, 2019.

Study of the labor and human costs of content moderation, both manual and automated.

Gorwa, Robert, Reuben Binns, and Christian Katzenbach. "Algorithmic Content Moderation: Technical and Political Challenges in the Automation of Platform Governance." Big Data & Society 7.1 (2020).

Analysis of technical and governance challenges in automated content moderation.

AI Ethics and Legal Theory

Hildebrandt, Mireille. Law for Computer Scientists and Other Folk. Oxford University Press, 2020.

Bridges computer science and legal theory, exploring how computational systems interact with legal norms.

Wachter, Sandra, Brent Mittelstadt, and Luciano Floridi. "Why a Right to Explanation of Automated Decision-Making Does Not Exist in the General Data Protection Regulation." International Data Privacy Law 7.2 (2017): 76-99.

Analysis of GDPR's transparency and explanation requirements for automated decisions.

Selbst, Andrew D., et al. "Fairness and Abstraction in Sociotechnical Systems." FAT* '19: Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (2019): 59-68.

Argues that technical fairness interventions fail when abstracted from social context.

About This Site

This website was created as part of the Humanities in the Age of AI course at the University of Central Florida (UCF). The project explores the intersection of artificial intelligence and legal practice, with particular focus on digital law and intellectual property.

All content is for educational purposes. While every effort has been made to ensure accuracy, this site is not legal advice and should not be relied upon as such. Laws and regulations are subject to change, and specific legal questions require consultation with qualified legal professionals.
